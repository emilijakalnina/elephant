A Tribute to Elephants

Gentle giants of the animal kingdom

Elephants are the largest land animals on Earth, and they have fascinated humans for thousands of years. Known for their intelligence, strong family bonds, and remarkable memory, elephants are found across Africa and Asia in a variety of habitats, from dense forests to open savannas.

There are three living species of elephant: the African bush elephant, the African forest elephant, and the Asian elephant. Each species has distinct physical features, including differences in ear size, tusk shape, and overall body size, but all share the same iconic trunk, which is used for breathing, smelling, drinking, and grabbing objects.

Sadly, elephants face serious threats from poaching and habitat loss. Conservation efforts around the world are working to protect these incredible animals so that future generations can continue to admire them in the wild. To learn more about ongoing conservation work, check out the World Wildlife Fund's elephant page.

Amazing Elephant Facts

  • Elephants can weigh up to 6,000 kg.
  • Their trunks contain over 40,000 muscles.
  • Elephants can live up to 70 years in the wild.
  • They communicate using low-frequency rumbles that can travel for miles.
